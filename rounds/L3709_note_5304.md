# DCF L3709: Gary's 5,304 observation. Three near-repdigits come from one identity, 444·12 = 666·8 = 888·6 = 5,328 = 144·37
### Gary NOMEREMORTAL Mason · Digital Cosmos Framework · 16 November 2026

**Gary's observation:** 5,304 ÷ 12 = 442, ÷ 8 = 663, ÷ 6 = 884, ÷ 4 = 1,326, ÷ 2 = 2,652. These come close to the triple repdigits 444, 666 and 888, which are multiples of 37.

**Arithmetic, checked in Python:** all five divisions are correct.

## The unifying identity
**444 × 12 = 666 × 8 = 888 × 6 = 1,332 × 4 = 2,664 × 2 = 5,328 = 2⁴ · 3² · 37 = 144 · 37.**

**5,304 = 5,328 − 24.** So 5,304 ÷ d = 5,328 ÷ d − 24 ÷ d:

| d | 5,304 ÷ d | nearby multiple of 37 | gap = 24 ÷ d |
|---|---|---|---|
| 12 | 442 | 444 = 12 · 37 | 2 |
| 8 | 663 | 666 = 18 · 37 | 3 |
| 6 | 884 | 888 = 24 · 37 | 4 |
| 4 | 1,326 | 1,332 = 36 · 37 | 6 |
| 2 | 2,652 | 2,664 = 72 · 37 | 12 |

- **The three near-repdigits are one fact seen five ways:** 5,304 lies 24 below 144 · 37.
- **The gaps 2, 3, 4, 6, 12 are 24 divided by each divisor.**

## How unusual is it?
- **Share of integers within ±24 of a multiple of 5,328:** 49 / 5,328 ≈ **0.9%.** So for one pre-chosen number, this closeness is uncommon.
- **Two cautions:**
  1. **Look-elsewhere.** The observation was found after looking at 5,304. With many possible targets (any repdigit family, any multiple of 37, other divisors), some near-miss is expected for almost any number.
  2. **5,304 is not a constant of nature.** It counts loop pairs whose anchor intervals match *exactly* in two files. It depends on bin sizes and calling methods (L3705). At a 2-kb tolerance, the matching count becomes 22,955, and other choices give other numbers.
- **Also note:**
  - 5,304 itself is 2³ · 3 · 13 · 17. **It is not a multiple of 37** (5,304 = 37 · 143 + 13).
  - Its nearest multiple of 37 is 5,291, 13 below; the 5,328 link is to the multiple 24 above.

**Status:** **verified arithmetic, with no statistical weight.** The identity 444 · 12 = 666 · 8 = 888 · 6 = 144 · 37 is a true and tidy fact about repdigits (each aaa = a · 111 = a · 3 · 37). It can be recorded in the DCF number notes.

## Summary
- **Gary's five divisions are correct.** Their closeness to 444, 666 and 888 comes from one identity: 12 · 444 = 8 · 666 = 6 · 888 = 5,328 = 144 · 37, and 5,304 sits 24 below it.
- **This is a neat, verified arithmetic fact.** But 5,304 is a method-dependent count of loop pairs, not a natural constant, so the closeness carries no biological meaning.
