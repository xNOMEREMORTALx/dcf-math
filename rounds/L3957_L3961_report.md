## The problem this raised, and L3961
**The reference (H1 + HFFc6 CTCF anchors) is where the K⁺ excess was first found.** Restricting any other map to loops at those anchors can import the excess by construction. This applies to L3909, L3913, L3953 and L3957.

**L3961 control:** rerun with a different reference, the union of the five T-cell ChIA-PET anchor sets. In that reference, pore K⁺ genes sit at anchors *less* often than matched genes (0.506 vs 0.542), so it cannot import an excess.

| run | maps | pore K⁺ share vs matched | p | result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z1 | 9 Hi-C maps (motor neurons, astrocytes, colon, muscle) | **0.173 vs 0.111** | **0.005** | **PASS** |
| Z2 | 2 T-cell Hi-C maps | 0.329 vs 0.416 | 0.96 (p_lower 0.049) | **FAIL** |

## What it means (the fixed interpretations)
**Z1 passes:** the excess in neurons, astrocytes, colon and muscle is **not** an artefact of the H1/HFFc6 reference.
- **It holds even with a reference that leans the other way.** §3.17 stands, and is strengthened.

**Z2 fails:** the T-cell Hi-C excess (L3953) and the restricted T-cell ChIA-PET excess (L3957) may be reference-inherited, so both are **downgraded**.
- **At CTCF sites shared with stem cells and fibroblasts,** T-cell loops do carry the K⁺ genes.
- **At T-cell CTCF ChIA-PET sites,** they do not; the genes sit below matched genes.

**So the T-cell question is open again:**
- **Two references, two answers.** The claim "the T-cell exception was an assay effect" (L3953) is withdrawn to "unresolved".
- **The cell-type reading is not restored either.** It cannot be, without CTCF data from T cells that does not depend on loop calls (CTCF ChIP-seq peaks).
